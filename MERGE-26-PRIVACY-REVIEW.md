# Merge 26 privacy and security review — owner decision draft

Reviewed against development code, disposable database tests and development-only browser tests on 4 November 2026. This is a technical assessment and proposed processing record, **not a legal-compliance certification**. Production data, configuration, processor contracts and production retention jobs were not inspected. No production release is authorised.

## Processing inventory

“Service basis” below means a proposed Article 6(1)(b) basis only where processing is objectively necessary to provide the requested service. Otherwise assess Article 6(1)(f) with a documented legitimate-interests assessment (LIA). The owner must approve purpose, necessity, balancing and transparency; user action is not automatically GDPR consent. This follows the distinction in the [ICO lawful-basis guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/).

Public numbers are **pseudonymous personal data**, not anonymity. Their persistence permits cross-game correlation, and public narrative text can itself identify someone. The [ICO pseudonymisation guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/pseudonymisation/) is relevant; removing emails from a label does not remove data-protection obligations.

## PECR and device storage

- Optional analytics uses `localStorage` for the dated allow/refuse choice and `sessionStorage` for the random tab session/context. No analytics request is generated before opt-in; refusal and withdrawal suppress future requests. These behaviours were exercised in the development browser and route tests.
- Session count means approximate consenting browser-tab visits, not unique people. Admin activity is suppressed using verified authentication, not a spoofable client flag. Anonymous internal testing cannot always be distinguished; no fingerprinting or hidden IP matching was added.
- First-party or cookieless measurement is not automatically exempt. The [ICO exceptions guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-exceptions/) describes a narrow statistical-purpose exception with transparency and objection requirements. No exception is assumed here, particularly for mixed acquisition purposes.
- Existing authentication, return-to-position state and locally saved feedback drafts are separate from visitor analytics. Assess necessity, scope and expiry rather than attaching analytics consent to all functionality.
- Optional video providers still require the existing click/consent mechanism. No third-party player or new tracking SDK is loaded by these additions. Supabase/Render hosting and Auth logs need a separate processor/transfer and cookie configuration review; this assessment did not inspect production infrastructure.

## Access, retention and individual rights findings

1. New mappings/state tables deny direct anonymous/ordinary access. Own waiting rows are readable only under recipient RLS; preference mutations validate ownership/eligibility server-side. Public identity output is report-scoped and minimised; direct canonical private-UUID rank lookup was revoked. Admin analytics checks both HTTP authorisation and DB service-only grants.
2. Canonical rank/XP decisions and historical reports are preserved. No AI summaries, individual public leaderboard, new developer analytics or automated issue extraction were added.
3. New 30-day waiting and 90-day availability-alert periods are technically enforced by the scheduled processor. Existing event expiry uses server timestamps at claim/submission time; scheduled delivery does not change feedback or drafts. The scheduler is enabled only in development. Production operation is a release gate.
4. Account deletion remains a **request** workflow. Nulling the public-number mapping and control-audit actors is not proof of complete account erasure: existing reports, XP/moderation history, contacts, support records, backups and caches require an operator procedure and a reasoned retention/restriction decision. Do not promise automatic complete erasure or irreversible anonymisation.
5. Before production, finalise a retention schedule for historical reports, existing notifications/delivery ledgers, public identity tombstones/timestamps, control/moderation audit and provider logs/backups. Existing policy drafts contain proposed periods, not evidence that every deletion job exists. Do not silently delete genuine history to make those drafts true.
6. Access/correction/erasure/restriction/objection/portability requests should use existing Contact/Support with proportionate verification, not passwords or service keys. Document applicable rights/exceptions, response responsibilities and any public-label suppression needed during a genuine restriction request. Current automated suppression is tied to missing/deleted profiles, not a complete restriction-request system; operator handling must be agreed before release.

## DPIA screening and accountability

This release adds persistent public pseudonyms and aggregated administration, not a new individual behavioural score or materially changed XP policy. Public correlation, harassment, mistaken association, children’s access and retention are meaningful risks. Controls include minimal public fields, accepted-feedback gating, current-rank labels, private mappings, human moderation/appeals and no new covert tracking.

The current legal UI describes an 18+ service, but actual audience/enforcement, scale, vulnerable users, systematic monitoring, combination with outside data and the significance of automatic eligibility decisions have **not** been established. The owner should record an Article 35 screening decision; commission a full DPIA if applicable high-risk criteria are met. Do not infer “DPIA not required” from passing tests. Review Article 22 applicability and meaningful human challenge if eligibility materially affects people. Record relevant processing under Article 30 where applicable, processor instructions/contracts, access reviews, breach handling and organisational security under Article 32. This technical review cannot establish all UK GDPR/DPA 2018 duties are met.

## Proposed notice additions — draft, not published approval

### Privacy: tester feedback and identities

“Accepted feedback displayed publicly may include a persistent Playtested tester number and your current tester rank. The same number can appear on feedback for different games, so other people may connect those contributions. This is a pseudonym, not a promise of anonymity. Your account email and private account identifier are not included in the public label. Pending and rejected reports remain restricted to authorised workflows. Authorised administrators and relevant developers can access the reports needed to operate and review testing. Rank labels show current status, not necessarily rank when the report was written.”

Add the **approved** lawful basis, purposes, retention periods, public recipients and objection/restriction/deletion arrangements before publishing. Explain the change to existing testers before enabling persistent public attribution; assess reasonable expectations and whether another basis or additional choice is needed.

### Privacy: administration and availability requests

“Administrators use restricted aggregate tester statistics to understand participation, testing durations and review workload. These statistics do not create a public tester leaderboard. If you request a place-availability alert, we store your account and campaign reference for up to 30 days and send an in-app bell alert if you remain eligible when a place becomes available. You can cancel the request in Tester Hub. Claiming a place cancels that campaign’s request. Alerts do not reserve a place and are not marketing or email subscriptions. These availability notifications are normally removed after 90 days; other service notification and audit retention is described separately.”

### Terms and cookie/privacy information

Explain that accepted public contributions can carry a stable pseudonymous number/current rank, that existing report access and moderation/appeals continue, that availability is first-come and may be gone when an alert is opened, and that an opt-in creates no entitlement to a reservation. Keep optional analytics off unless allowed, identify the actual browser storage/durations and withdrawal control, and distinguish these from necessary account and draft/return-position functionality. Keep provider-player consent and private contact preferences separate. Do not imply availability requests authorise promotional contact.
