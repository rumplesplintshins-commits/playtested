# Merge 26 privacy operations - proposal/evidence, not sign-off

## Existing deletion workflow and verified blockers

Account /account/delete calls request_account_deletion and stores a staged request. It does not sign out or perform erasure. Read-only production evidence confirms community_game_tests.tester_id is NOT NULL and references auth.users ON DELETE CASCADE. test_review_events.test_id and developer_feedback_signals.test_id reference reports without cascading deletion. The submitted_place_integrity trigger calls guard_submitted_test, which rejects deletion of submitted/reviewed reports and freezes participation identity. Auth deletion can also cascade developer submissions, campaigns, builds, ownerships and other records, and meet non-cascading reviewer/contact references. It can therefore either destroy dependent history or fail; neither outcome is an approved erasure procedure. Merge26 identity/control-audit SET NULL paths do not repair these older dependencies.

### Proposed manual request procedure for approval

1. Receive through /account/delete or monitored support contact; log receipt, scope, responsible operator and response due date privately, not in Git. Use proportionate verification through the existing account channel; never request passwords, API keys or unnecessary identity documents. An acknowledgement is not completion.
2. Assess objection/restriction/erasure separately, including interim protection where appropriate. Do not presume a technical FK or a report's usefulness overrides a person's rights. Track applicable response times; use a one-calendar-month default response workflow and obtain advice before relying on extensions, pauses, refusal or retention exceptions.
3. Inventory Auth identities/sessions, profiles, PC specs, optional contacts, developer relationships, submitted/free text, game files, reports, XP/moderation events, reviewer references, notifications/dedupe, public mapping/tombstones, analytics, support records, provider logs and backups. Identify controller versus independent developer/provider data and recipients to notify where applicable.
4. Record a purpose/necessity decision for each category: erase, restrict, retain for a documented reason/review date or de-identify. Review free text and timestamps as well as identifiers; do not call pseudonymisation irreversible anonymity. Do not promise immediate provider-backup erasure without evidence.
5. For users with genuine submitted reports or developer history, STOP before auth.admin.deleteUser. Current schema/guards do not provide a tested general detach/de-identify operation. Choose/review and rehearse a narrow privileged restriction/de-identification design preserving report/audit and XP integrity. Do not disable guards, cascade-delete reports, create a fake shared tester or reset scores as a shortcut. No destructive SQL is provided or executed here.
6. Execute an approved plan only with correct permissions, a recovery point and a reversible scoped change where appropriate; verify public and private views, session revocation and removal of personal fields. Preserve justified audit continuity with minimal identifying data. A soft-delete/request alone is not proof of erasure. Document any residual retention and provider limitations.
7. Give the person the reasoned outcome and complaint/reconsideration route; schedule follow-up/review for retained data and preserve only the minimum request record. Do not certify completion while technical blockers remain.

A process for intake/assessment is available; a tested general completion path is NOT. Owner must appoint the operator and approve the design before relying on it in a final notice.

## Public-number objection/restriction gap

Current code exposes public numbers/current ranks only through eligible accepted-feedback resolution; admins have separate private access. The number is persistent across games. Contact/Support can receive objections/restriction requests, but there is no dedicated per-tester public-attribution suppression control. Missing/deleted profiles are not a restriction feature. Removing a mapping risks later reallocation; changing acceptance or XP to hide a label is inappropriate.

Proposed implementation for review: a private, audited admin-managed public-attribution restriction flag respected by every public number/rank resolver, leaving genuine report outcomes, XP and authorised private moderation unchanged. Include interim restrictions, review dates and tests for all public surfaces, legacy accepted feedback, re-enabling and no cross-account access. Until chosen and verified, do not promise immediate automated suppression or treat an email request as completion. Alternatively defer enabling new persistent public attribution; that would require an explicit scoped release decision and implementation, not a silent omission.

## Consent implementation: works versus evidence gap

Verified source/tests: page analytics checks explicit allowed plus 180-day age; Steam additionally requires steam_referrals=true, so old generic Allow does not enable it. Both respect fresh refusal/withdrawal and DNT/GPC; server validates consent:true and trusted origin. Steam validates authentication/owned history and excludes admins. Navigation is direct and independent. No account/report/session/IP/UA field is stored in Steam events.

Limitations: the UI has a combined explained Allow/Reject, not independent per-purpose controls. Local preference stores flags/time; it has no notice version and may be erased. Requests carry a boolean, not a verifiable human-action proof. There is no durable scope/version/withdrawal receipt or complete archived notice-to-choice linkage. Event timestamps alone do not establish what a person saw or chose.

Owner decision: assess whether the proposed consent arrangement is demonstrable and sufficiently granular. Recommended minimal work if not: separate purpose choices, versioned notice/scope/time evidence and archived text/tests, with a proportionate receipt design that does not introduce account/device linkage or fingerprinting. A centrally identifiable ledger is not automatically required or desirable; do not collect extra identifiers just to make analytics linkable. If necessary evidence cannot be established, keep optional collection disabled until corrected under an approved design. No lawful-basis switch or exception is silently substituted. [ICO consent records](https://ico.org.uk/media/for-organisations/guide-to-the-general-data-protection-regulation-gdpr/consent-1-0.pdf).

## Preliminary DPIA screening - outcome NOT DETERMINED

Observed current production aggregates: 44 profiles (not a complete Auth-account count), 40 report rows, 32 accepted. These counts do not establish future scale, audience or absence of vulnerability. New persistent cross-game pseudonyms can enable correlation/harassment; current rank and automated eligibility can affect access; optional analytics can potentially correlate with operational logs. Free text can contain sensitive material even though it is not requested. PC specs are voluntary/private model data, not device inspection. Human moderation/appeal, accepted-only public gating, private mapping/RLS and consent-off defaults mitigate but do not settle the assessment.

Owner must record: intended audience and age-assurance/legacy-user handling; vulnerable users; current/planned scale and retention; public correlation and outside-data combination; whether ranking/eligibility has legal or similarly significant effects and meaningful human challenge; sensitive/free-text risk; necessity/proportionality and residual risk. Decide whether full DPIA is required under current criteria; if likely high risk, complete the applicable assessment before starting the processing and address any unresolved high risk appropriately. Do not label this a completed DPIA or infer Article22 inapplicable merely because XP is not money. Also assign processing-record, access-review/security, incident/rights-response and provider-contract responsibilities where applicable.
